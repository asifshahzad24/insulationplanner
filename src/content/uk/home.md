## How the calculator works

Building Regulations in England set insulation targets as **U-values**: how many watts of heat pass through one square metre of roof, wall or floor for each degree of temperature difference. Lower is better. The calculator works backwards from the U-value you choose to the insulation you need.

1. **Pick the area.** Loft, room in roof, cavity walls, solid walls or a suspended timber floor. This page opens on a loft with mineral wool loft roll.
2. **Measure it.** Enter length × width or a total area. For walls you can enter the wall length and height, and the calculator takes off 1.9 m² per door and 1.5 m² per window.
3. **Tell it what's there now.** A depth and type of insulation, or an R-value or U-value if you know it.
4. **Choose how far to go.** **Building Regs (Part L)** uses the values for upgrading an existing element. **New-build standard** uses the stricter values for a new element.
5. **Choose a product.** Loft roll, PIR board, EPS board, or installer-blown mineral wool, depending on the area.

### The U-value formula

The calculator uses the standard method from BS EN ISO 6946, with the conventions in BRE's BR 443:

**U = 1 ÷ (Rsi + Σ thickness/λ + Rse)**

- **Rsi and Rse** are the thin layers of still air on the inside and outside surfaces, with fixed values set by the standard.
- **thickness/λ** is the R-value of each layer: its thickness in metres divided by its thermal conductivity, λ (lambda). Everything else in the element, such as plasterboard, brick or a ventilated void, adds a fixed R-value too.

For a loft, 1 ÷ 0.16 gives a total R of 6.25 m²K/W. About 0.57 of that comes from the surfaces and the ceiling, so the insulation has to supply **R 5.68 m²K/W**.

### Joists, rafters and λ

Insulation laid between timber joists doesn't work as well as the same thickness on its own, because heat also flows through the wood. The calculator allows for this (a parallel-path calculation), which is why you lay the first layer of loft roll between the joists and the **second layer across them**, where nothing bridges it.

λ matters too. The loft roll in our data, Knauf Loft Roll 44, has λ 0.044 W/m·K. A roll with λ 0.040 needs about 9% less thickness for the same R-value, so check the λ on the pack you buy.

## Before you start

- **Building Regulations.** If you renovate more than half of a roof, wall or floor, or more than a quarter of the whole envelope, Approved Document L expects the element to be upgraded. Ask building control or your installer if you're unsure.
- **Vermiculite.** Pebbly loose fill in a loft may be vermiculite, which can contain asbestos. Don't disturb it; see the HSE guidance below.
- **Scotland and Wales.** Both set their own targets. Check your nation's guidance.
- **Check the pack.** Roll and board sizes vary by brand. If yours differ, enter your own price under **More options** and check the coverage.

## How to read the table

Each row of the table above is an **R-value** in m²K/W, from R 1 to R 8. Each column is a type of insulation, with its thermal conductivity, **λ (lambda)**, in W/m·K. The cell shows how thick that product has to be to reach that R-value:

**Thickness (mm) = R × λ × 1000**

The lower the λ, the less thickness you need. Under each R-value you'll also see **U ≈ 1 ÷ R**. That's the U-value of the insulation layer on its own, not of the whole loft or wall, which also includes surface resistances, the ceiling or masonry, and any timber joists. The [U-value calculator](/uk/u-value-calculator) explains the difference.

The columns are:

| Column | λ (W/m·K) | Used for |
|---|---|---|
| Mineral wool loft roll | 0.044 | Lofts and suspended floors |
| PIR board | 0.022 | Solid walls, floors, rooms in the roof |
| EPS board | 0.038 | Floors and solid walls |
| Blown (installer), cavity fill | 0.040 | Cavity walls |
| Blown (installer), loft | 0.045 | Lofts |

Two columns are labelled **Blown (installer)**: the λ 0.040 one is the cavity wall fill, and the λ 0.045 one is blown loft insulation.

## Why 270 mm?

The Energy Saving Trust recommends at least 270 mm of mineral wool in a loft. Here's how that lines up with the Building Regulations.

1. **The target.** Approved Document L asks for U-value 0.16 W/m²K when you upgrade a loft.
2. **The R the insulation must supply.** A U-value of 0.16 means a total R of 1 ÷ 0.16 = 6.25 m²K/W. The surfaces and the ceiling provide about 0.57 of that, so the insulation needs **R 5.68**.
3. **The thickness on paper.** R 5.68 × λ 0.044 = **250 mm** of loft roll.
4. **The joists.** The first layer sits between timber joists, which conduct heat about three times better than mineral wool. That costs about 20 mm, so in practice you need **270 mm**: 100 mm between the joists and 170 mm across them.

Knauf and the RdSAP 10 specification both give U-value 0.16 for that 100 + 170 mm build-up, and the loft calculator is calibrated to it.

## Worked example: how much to add

Most lofts already have some insulation. For a 45 m² loft with old mineral wool, the [loft insulation calculator](/uk/loft-insulation-calculator) returns these top-ups to reach U-value 0.16, with 10% waste:

| Depth there now | U-value now | Add | Rolls of Loft Roll 44 | U-value after |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| None | 1.75 | 100 + 170 mm | 4 + 7 | 0.16 |
| 50 mm | 0.65 | 150 + 100 mm | 6 + 4 | 0.14 |
| 100 mm | 0.40 | 170 mm | 7 | 0.16 |
| 120 mm | 0.33 | 150 mm | 6 | 0.16 |
| 200 mm | 0.20 | 100 mm | 4 | 0.14 |
| 270 mm | 0.15 | Nothing needed | – | 0.15 |

Rolls only come in 100, 150, 170 and 200 mm, so the total doesn't always land on exactly 270 mm. The calculator picks the fewest layers, then the thinnest stack, that reaches the target. Existing mineral wool is valued at λ 0.040, the figure RdSAP 10 uses.

If you'd go further, the **New-build standard** of U-value 0.15 takes 300 mm in an empty loft: 200 mm plus 100 mm of roll, reaching 0.14.

## λ 0.044 or λ 0.040 roll?

Loft rolls are sold with different λ values. Our data uses Knauf Loft Roll 44, at λ 0.044. A λ 0.040 roll reaches the same R-value in about 9% less thickness, which can help where space is tight. What counts is the R-value of the layer, so compare products by the R-value of the thickness you'll lay, not by thickness alone. The same goes for boards: PIR at λ 0.022 needs half the thickness of λ 0.044 roll, and EPS at λ 0.038 sits in between.

## Things that cut real thickness

- **Compression.** Boards, boxes and walkways laid straight on the joists squash the insulation. Use loft legs.
- **Thin spots.** Insulation is often thinner at the eaves, around the hatch and near the water tank. Measure in several places, not just one.
- **Gaps.** Rolls that don't meet, or that stop short of the joists, leave paths for heat. Butt the edges together.
- **Blocking the eaves.** Don't solve a thin spot by stuffing insulation into the eaves. The loft needs that ventilation.

For other areas of the house, see [Part L U-values](/uk/part-l-u-values) for the targets, or size a job with the [solid wall insulation calculator](/uk/solid-wall-insulation-calculator).

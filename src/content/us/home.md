## How the calculator works

The calculator answers one question: how much insulation do you need to add to reach the level recommended for your climate? It works in five steps.

1. **Pick the area.** Attic, walls, floor, basement walls or a sloped roof. This page opens on an attic floor with blown cellulose, but you can switch to any of them.
2. **Measure it.** Enter length × width or a total area. For walls, enter the perimeter and wall height, and the calculator subtracts doors and windows.
3. **Tell us what's there now.** Enter the depth and type of the old insulation, or its R-value if you know it.
4. **Enter your ZIP code.** We look up your 2021 IECC climate zone and the R-value ENERGY STAR recommends for that zone.
5. **Choose a material.** Blown cellulose, blown fiberglass, batts, foam board or spray foam, depending on the area.

The core formula is simple:

**R to add = target R − R you already have**

Your existing R comes from depth × a per-inch value. ENERGY STAR publishes these for older insulation: 3.2 per inch for fiberglass batts, 2.5 for loose fiberglass, 3.7 for cellulose, 2.8 for loose rock wool and 2.7 for vermiculite. The calculator then turns the R you need into a depth and a count of bags or sheets, using each manufacturer's coverage figures. It adds 10% for waste by default. You can change that under **More options**.

## Worked example: a 1,000 sq ft attic in zone 4A

Say your attic floor is 1,000 sq ft, your ZIP code puts you in climate zone 4A, and there's about 6 in of old loose fiberglass up there.

- Existing insulation: 6 in × 2.5 per inch = **R-15**
- Target for a zone 4A attic: **R-60**
- R to add: **R-45**
- Area to cover with 10% waste: **1,100 sq ft**

Here's what the calculator returns for three materials:

| Material | Add | To buy | Material cost |
|---|---|---|---|
| Blown cellulose (25 lb bags) | 13.2 in, settles to 12.3 in | 72 bags | $1,151–$1,294 |
| Blown fiberglass (28.6 lb bags) | 15.2 in | 27 bags | $1,169–$1,313 |
| Fiberglass batts, 24 in wide | One layer of R-49 (14 in) | 23 bags | $2,461 |

Two things stand out. Blown fiberglass needs more depth than cellulose for the same R-value, but far fewer bags, because each bag covers much more floor. And batts only come in set sizes, so the smallest single layer that reaches the target, R-49, takes you to R-64 rather than exactly R-60.

Costs are for materials only, at typical retail prices from October 2026. They don't include tools, labor or the blower.

## Before you buy

- **Check the bag.** Every loose-fill product has its own coverage chart. Our numbers come from GreenFiber and Knauf charts. If you buy another brand, check our bag count against its chart.
- **Seal air leaks first.** Gaps around wiring, plumbing, chimneys and the attic hatch let warm air slip past the insulation. Seal them before new insulation covers them.
- **Watch for vermiculite.** Pebbly, gray-brown loose fill may contain asbestos. The EPA advises leaving it undisturbed and having a professional deal with it.
- **Check your local code** if the job needs a permit. Your state or city may use a different edition of the energy code, or amend it.

## How to read the chart

Each column is one type of insulation and each row is a target R-value. The cell shows how thick that material has to be to reach it:

**Depth (in) = R-value ÷ R per inch**

Under each material name you'll see λ (lambda), its thermal conductivity in W/m·K. That's the metric way of saying the same thing: the lower the λ, the less thickness you need. To switch between the two, **R per inch ≈ 0.1442 ÷ λ**. The [R-value to U-value converter](/r-value-to-u-value-converter) does this for any thickness.

| Column | R per inch | λ (W/m·K) |
|---|---|---|
| Fiberglass batts (wall grade) | 3.7 | 0.039 |
| Fiberglass batts (attic and floor) | 3.2 | 0.045 |
| Mineral wool batts | 4.2 | 0.034 |
| Blown cellulose | 3.65 | 0.040 |
| Blown fiberglass | 2.92 | 0.049 |
| Polyiso board | 6 | 0.024 |
| XPS board | 5 | 0.029 |
| EPS board | 3.9 | 0.037 |
| Open-cell spray foam | 3.7 | 0.039 |
| Closed-cell spray foam | 6.5 | 0.022 |

Fiberglass batts appear twice because wall batts and attic batts are made differently. Wall batts are made to pack more R into a 3.5 in or 5.5 in stud cavity.

## Worked example: how thick is R-38?

Reading across the R-38 row:

| Material | Depth for R-38 |
|---|---|
| Closed-cell spray foam | 5.85 in |
| Polyiso board | 6.33 in |
| XPS board | 7.6 in |
| Mineral wool batts | 9.05 in |
| EPS board | 9.74 in |
| Wall fiberglass or open-cell foam | 10.3 in |
| Blown cellulose | 10.4 in |
| Attic fiberglass batts | 11.9 in |
| Blown fiberglass | 13 in |

Now compare with real products. The R-38 fiberglass attic batt in our data is 12 in thick. GreenFiber's chart puts R-38 of cellulose at 10.4 in settled, installed at 11.2 in. Knauf's chart puts R-38 of blown fiberglass at 13 in. For R-38, chart and products line up closely.

## When to trust the label instead

The chart uses one R per inch for each material. Real products vary a little, and for anything you're buying, **the label or coverage chart wins**.

- **Batts come in fixed sizes.** The R-49 attic batt in our data is 14 in thick, not the 15.3 in the 3.2-per-inch column suggests, because the maker packs more R into each inch of the thicker batt. Buy by the R on the label.
- **Loose-fill settles.** The chart shows settled depth. Cellulose is blown deeper and settles down to it: for R-60, GreenFiber's chart says install 17.3 in and it settles to 16.1 in.
- **Coverage charts aren't perfectly linear.** For R-60, Knauf's chart gives 19.75 in of blown fiberglass, a little less than the 20.5 in in our column. The [blown-in insulation calculator](/blown-in-insulation-calculator) uses the manufacturer chart, so its numbers can differ slightly from this page.

## Checking what you have now

Old insulation doesn't perform like new product. To estimate the R-value of what's already in your attic, ENERGY STAR gives these per-inch values: fiberglass batts 3.2, loose fiberglass 2.5, cellulose 3.7, rock wool 2.8 and vermiculite 2.7. For example, 6 in of old loose fiberglass is about R-15, even though new blown fiberglass rates 2.92 per inch.

If what you find is pebbly vermiculite, don't disturb it. The EPA warns it may contain asbestos.

## Thickness isn't everything

- **Compression** cuts R. A batt squeezed into a shallow space ends up below its label.
- **Gaps and air leaks** let heat bypass the insulation. Seal before you insulate.
- **Framing** conducts more heat than insulation. The R-values here are for the insulation alone, the way US targets are written.

Once you know the depth, plan the quantity with the [attic insulation calculator](/attic-insulation-calculator), and check your zone's target on [insulation R-values by climate zone](/insulation-by-climate-zone).

## How the wall calculation works

### 1. Measure the net wall area

Choose **Measure walls** and enter the total length of the exterior walls (the house perimeter), the wall height, and how many doors and windows there are. The calculator works out:

**Net wall area = perimeter × wall height − (doors × 21 sq ft) − (windows × 12 sq ft)**

Those opening sizes are typical defaults. If you have big patio doors or picture windows, measure them and use **Total area** instead.

### 2. Find your target

For walls, the calculator uses the cavity-only option from the 2021 IECC, which ENERGY STAR's table is based on:

| Climate zone | Wall target |
|---|---|
| 1–2 | R-13 |
| 3 | R-20 |
| 4–8 | R-30 |

### 3. Check what fits

This is where walls differ from attics. Insulation between the studs can't be deeper than the studs. A 2×4 wall has a 3.5 in cavity and a 2×6 wall has 5.5 in. Pick your stud size under **Cavity depth** and the calculator caps the in-wall insulation at that depth. If the target doesn't fit, it shows the best you can get and warns you.

Wall batts come sized for the cavity: R-13 and R-15 are 3.5 in thick for 2×4s, and R-21 is 5.5 in for 2×6s. The calculator uses 15 in wide batts, made for studs 16 in on center.

## Worked example: a one-story house in zone 4A

A 50 × 30 ft house has a 160 ft perimeter. With 9 ft walls, 2 doors and 10 windows:

- Gross wall area: 160 × 9 = 1,440 sq ft
- Minus 2 doors (42 sq ft) and 10 windows (120 sq ft): **1,278 sq ft net**
- With 10% waste: **1,406 sq ft**
- Zone 4A target: **R-30**

| Wall and product | Result | To buy | Cost |
|---|---|---|---|
| 2×4, fiberglass batts | R-15 (cavity full) | 21 bags of R-15 | $980–$1,438 |
| 2×6, fiberglass batts | R-21 (cavity full) | 21 bags of R-21 | $1,818–$2,044 |
| 2×6, closed-cell spray foam | R-30 at 4.62 in | Installed by a pro | $8,848–$14,746 |

Batts fall short of R-30 in both walls, and the calculator says so. Closed-cell spray foam gets there inside a 2×6, but its price is an installed cost for a professional job, and spray contractors often have a minimum charge.

The same 2×6 wall with R-21 batts in zone 3A meets that zone's R-20 target with the same 21 bags.

## Why R-30 walls need continuous insulation

R-30 doesn't fit in a standard 2×4 or 2×6 cavity with batts. The usual answer is **continuous insulation**: rigid foam board over the outside of the studs, normally added when the siding comes off. Because it isn't interrupted by studs, the code accepts lower cavity-plus-board combinations as equal to R-30 cavity-only. The [PNNL table of IECC requirements](https://basc.pnnl.gov/information/2009-2021-iecc-and-irc-minimum-insulation-requirements-new-homes) lists the combinations for each zone.

You can model this in two passes. First size the cavity insulation. Then set **What's there now** to "I know the R-value", enter the cavity R, and choose foam board. For the 2×6 wall above, entering R-21 and choosing polyiso gives one layer of 1.65 in R-10 board: **44 sheets, $1,649**, for a total of R-31. The calculator simply adds nominal R-values, so treat that as a conservative answer, not the code minimum.

## Dense-pack vs. batts

- **Open walls** (new framing or a gut renovation): batts are the DIY choice. Cut them to fit snugly with no gaps.
- **Closed walls**: an installer blows dense-pack cellulose or injects foam through holes drilled in the siding or drywall. At the R-3.65 per inch our cellulose data uses, a full 2×4 cavity works out to about R-13, so you get about the same R as a batt, just without opening the wall. The calculator doesn't model dense-pack yet.

## Tips and common mistakes

- **Don't compress batts.** Split a batt around wires and pipes instead of pushing it behind them.
- **Fill the whole cavity.** Cut batts to fit around electrical boxes and fill the narrow bays beside windows and corners.
- **Faced batts:** staple the flanges to the sides or faces of the studs, facing the heated room. Whether you need a vapor retarder at all depends on your climate zone, so check local code.
- **Match the width to the stud spacing.** 15 in batts fit studs 16 in on center; attic batts are wider.
- **Check for wiring problems** before closing a wall, and never bury old knob-and-tube wiring without an electrician's sign-off.
